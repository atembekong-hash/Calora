**Mission:** Calora Autonomous Production Certification Mission

**Audit mode:** bounded, read-only source/test/history review. No purchase, store mutation, provider mutation, production mutation, build, deployment, database write, credential access, or device operation was performed.

## Executive result

The protected source contains a comparatively careful RevenueCat integration: purchase and restore are blocked until the RevenueCat identity exactly matches the signed-in Calora account; entitlement checks use the exact `caloraapp_pro` key; client billing secrets are not used; offering mismatches and missing store prices disable purchasing; purchase/restore results are not represented as active unless the expected entitlement is returned; and account deletion has a fail-closed RevenueCat erasure adapter with post-delete absence verification. Public privacy, terms, subscription, support, and account-deletion pages are implemented, and wellness/medical disclaimers are present.

This evidence is not sufficient for a full US commercial launch certification. The repository’s own billing certification says **NOT CERTIFIED FOR LAUNCH** because native/store transactions and lifecycle events were not exercised, platform subscription management was historically incomplete, and paid Pro benefits were not fully enforced. Later source appears to improve native management URL handling and broader entitlement gates, but there is still no current protected-main evidence of real App Store or Google Play purchase, active restore, renewal, expiration, grace-period/billing-retry, cancellation/refund/revocation, cross-device restore, or account-switch behavior. Store metadata is a repository-controlled submission worksheet, not current App Store Connect/Play Console evidence. Native accessibility and store review evidence were not run in this audit.

## Critical findings and blockers

1. **Full commercial launch cannot be certified from current evidence.** The strongest historical billing document (`docs/calora-payment-billing-entitlements-certification.md`) explicitly concludes **NOT CERTIFIED FOR LAUNCH** and lists mandatory native/provider lifecycle work.
2. **The most important unverified paths are real-money and lifecycle paths:** new purchase, cancellation, active restore, renewal, expiration, grace/billing retry, refund/revocation, cross-device restore, and account switch. No purchase or provider state was initiated in this read-only audit.
3. **Store configuration evidence is absent.** App Store/Play files are controlled drafts/checklists with unchecked final console steps. There is no current console export or signed binary evidence tying products, trial eligibility, taxes, privacy declarations, reviewer access, and listing state to this baseline.
4. **Entitlement coverage remains a launch concern.** Premium recipe/API paths fail closed, but the historical certification specifically failed marketed-Pro enforcement. A current feature-by-feature entitlement inventory and post-expiry denial run is required before claiming paid-feature compliance.
5. **Support operations are not evidenced.** The public email and support pages exist, but mailbox delivery/reply and escalation/SLA execution were not verified; Play’s final checklist leaves that item open.
6. **Native accessibility is not certified.** Source and deterministic tests are useful evidence, but TalkBack/VoiceOver, dynamic type, purchase sheets, cancellation settings, and deletion flows on signed iOS/Android builds remain unrun.
