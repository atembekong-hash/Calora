MASTER MISSION: CALORAAPP 3D UI, MOTION, SPATIAL DESIGN, UX COMPRESSION, AND SAFE VISUAL MODERNIZATION

You are working on an existing production-bound application called CaloraApp.

This is NOT a greenfield redesign.

The application already contains substantial working functionality, business logic, navigation, integrations, data handling, authentication, subscriptions, nutrition features, planning features, progress tracking, scanning, recipes, CaloraApp Coach, profile/settings, and other interconnected systems.

Your mission is to substantially elevate the visual and interactive quality of the ENTIRE CaloraApp while preserving its existing capabilities and avoiding regressions.

The desired result is:

RICH WITHOUT LOOKING BUSY.

CaloraApp should become:
- More dimensional.
- More tactile.
- More elegant.
- More compact.
- More spatially intelligent.
- More animated.
- More visually distinctive.
- Easier to understand.
- Faster to operate.
- Less cluttered.
- Less repetitive.
- More premium.
- More emotionally satisfying to interact with.

The application must retain its functional depth.

DO NOT achieve simplicity by deleting useful functionality.

Achieve simplicity by improving:
- hierarchy,
- progressive disclosure,
- compact controls,
- grouping,
- navigation,
- information density,
- iconography,
- spatial organization,
- interaction design,
- motion,
- 3D treatment,
- contextual actions,
- and visual prioritization.

==================================================
0. PRIME DIRECTIVE: DO NO HARM
==================================================

Treat this as controlled modernization of a working system.

DO NOT begin by rewriting screens.

DO NOT perform a broad refactor merely because the existing architecture is imperfect.

DO NOT replace working infrastructure unless necessary for this mission.

DO NOT modify unrelated systems.

DO NOT delete existing features because they appear visually inconvenient.

DO NOT silently change business rules.

DO NOT silently change navigation behavior.

DO NOT silently change API contracts.

DO NOT silently change database schemas.

DO NOT silently change authentication.

DO NOT silently change authorization.

DO NOT silently change subscriptions, RevenueCat, purchase handling, entitlements, trials, pricing logic, or restore-purchase behavior.

DO NOT silently change Supabase configuration.

DO NOT silently change backend endpoints.

DO NOT silently change nutrition calculations.

DO NOT silently change user data structures.

DO NOT silently change analytics or telemetry behavior.

DO NOT silently change Coach/contextual-intelligence behavior.

DO NOT silently change health integrations.

DO NOT introduce new third-party dependencies simply to achieve a visual effect when the existing stack can reasonably provide it.

DO NOT trigger a production build, store submission, database migration, destructive command, production deployment, or irreversible infrastructure operation unless explicitly required and demonstrably safe.

Prefer additive, reversible changes.

Before touching any important shared component, determine everywhere it is used.

Before removing anything, prove that it is redundant.

Before changing shared design primitives, determine the blast radius.

Before changing navigation, identify every route that depends on it.

Before changing state management, determine all consumers.

Before changing a data-bound component, understand the data lifecycle.

When uncertainty exists, investigate rather than guess.

==================================================
4. DESIGN THESIS
==================================================

The guiding principle is:

FEATURE DENSITY WITHOUT VISUAL DENSITY.

A screen may contain substantial capability while presenting only the controls relevant to the current moment.

Reduce clutter through:
- hierarchy,
- grouping,
- compact controls,
- progressive disclosure,
- contextual actions,
- expandable surfaces,
- menus,
- intelligent defaults,
- sheets,
- compact action clusters,
- icon-first actions where understandable,
- and better information architecture.

Do NOT reduce clutter by simply removing features.

==================================================
5. COMPACT ACTION ARCHITECTURE
==================================================

A major inspiration is the efficient presentation used by polished financial applications where several actions such as Send, Deposit, Utilities, and More can occupy very little dashboard space while remaining immediately understandable.

Apply this principle appropriately to Calora.

Investigate where large Calora controls can become compact action systems.

Examples may include:

- Log food
- Scan
- Barcode
- Voice
- Water
- Exercise
- Saved meal
- Restaurant
- Quick add
- Create recipe
- Plan
- Shopping
- Coach
- Weight logging
- Health connection

Do NOT automatically place all these actions together.

Determine which actions belong on which screen.

Frequently used actions should remain immediately reachable.

Secondary actions can use progressive disclosure.

Rare actions should not compete visually with primary content.

A compact action system may use:
- dimensional icon buttons,
- compact labeled tiles,
- horizontal action rails,
- contextual floating controls,
- expandable action clusters,
- sheets,
- menus,
- or another coherent interaction pattern.

Keep touch targets accessible even when visual footprints become smaller.

Visual compactness must NOT produce tiny tap targets.

==================================================
10. MOTION HIERARCHY
==================================================

If everything moves dramatically, nothing feels important.

Therefore:

Frequent interactions:
small motion.

Context changes:
medium motion.

Important accomplishments:
larger motion.

Major moments:
rare cinematic motion.

Never let animation slow routine logging.

Users should be able to log food quickly.

Motion should usually happen alongside completion rather than forcing users to wait for it.

- startup time,
- navigation responsiveness,
- scrolling,
- memory usage,
- battery consumption,
- image loading,
- camera performance,
- lower-end device performance.

Measure rather than assuming performance is acceptable.

For normal implementation choices, investigate and continue.

For dangerous or irreversible choices, stop.
